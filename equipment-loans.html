Understanding Equipment Purchase Loans

An equipment loan is a traditional commercial term facility specifically structured to fund the purchase price of designated business assets. When funding occurs, the business assumes legal title to the equipment, and the lending institution places a UCC-1 security lien on the asset until the loan is satisfied in full.

This structure is favored by enterprises that intend to maintain and utilize the machinery for many years beyond the loan maturity horizon, eliminating return obligations associated with operating leases.

Repayment Term Horizons

Loan terms typically mirror the productive depreciation schedule of the equipment:

  • 24–36 Months: IT servers, diagnostic software, point of sale
  • 48–60 Months: Fleet delivery vehicles, kitchen equipment, tooling
  • 72–84 Months: Heavy earthmoving machinery, combine harvesters, plant lines
Security & Collateral

Because the purchased equipment functions as collateral:

  • Primary collateral is isolated to the specific asset
  • Avoids blanket liens on company accounts receivable
  • No requirement for residential real estate pledges in standard structures

New vs. Used Equipment Considerations

Lenders evaluate applications differently depending on whether the asset is brand new from a certified dealer or pre-owned from a third-party seller:

Factor New Equipment Used Equipment
Advance Rate Up to 100% invoice price Typically 75% – 90% of appraised orderly liquidation value
Maximum Term Up to 84 months Usually limited so age + term does not exceed 10–12 years
Verification Dealer invoice and warranty spec sheet Inspection report, serial log, maintenance history & bill of sale

Business Documentation Required for Underwriting

1. Equipment Vendor Invoice / Spec Quote Make, model, year, serial number, and full invoice breakdown from vendor.
2. Recent Business Banking History 3 to 6 months of complete operating bank statements showing cash flow.
3. Business Tax Returns Past 2 years of signed business federal tax returns (for loans over $150K).
4. Entity Legal Verification Articles of incorporation, operating agreement, and owner identification.
